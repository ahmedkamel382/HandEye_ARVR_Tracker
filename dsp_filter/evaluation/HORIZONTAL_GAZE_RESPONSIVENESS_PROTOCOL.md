# Horizontal gaze responsiveness, proposed protocol v1

**Status: predeclared for review, before any horizontal gaze responsiveness recording.** No live session is authorized by this document. Score LEFT to RIGHT and RIGHT to LEFT separately. A trial passes only when both directions pass in that recording. Retain every attempt. Freeze a copy and its digest before launch. If the method changes, issue another version before collecting new data.

## Question and setup

Measure the extra delay between a measured horizontal gaze coordinate entering Engineer 4 and its filtered coordinate submitted to Engineer 5. This does not measure stimulus-to-eye latency, physical eye speed, visual-target accuracy, or end-to-end cursor delay.

Use two identical stationary crosses centered at 25% and 75% of the same display width, both at 50% height. Keep the display, viewport, seating, face-camera distance, light and head support fixed. Log these, display size and any glasses. The screen fractions specify stimuli; they are not expected tracker coordinates. Look at LEFT before starting. Keep your head comfortable and still, and blink normally. On the first MOVE shift your eyes naturally to RIGHT and stay there. STOP means keep fixating there, even if you arrived before it. On the next MOVE shift back to LEFT; STOP means hold LEFT. No forced slow eye movement, deliberate wink, or hand signal.

**Proposed cue design:** one 32-second audio track, anchored only after readiness in a future live run. COUNT at 5, 6, 7 s; MOVE at 8 s and STOP at 9 s; COUNT at 17, 18, 19 s; MOVE at 20 s and STOP at 21 s. Hold through the end. The 1-second spacing communicates the hold instruction and imposes no minimum or maximum saccade duration. Rehearsal uses no tracker. In a separately approved live run use new external output, camera 0, DSP, hand disabled, gaze override 0.8,6,1.5, OS log, tray paused, and a 55-second recorder horizon. No debugger, real mouse, OS wizard, or setting changes.

## Calibration, clocks and direction

- Python FaceLandmarker provides 478 image points plus six packed pose-matrix rows. GazeNative automatically accumulates 30 face observations with both eye aspect ratios above 1e-6. State -1, X/Y = -1 on calibration frames, including the completing frame. While seated normally, let it calibrate in the recorded startup. No separate pre-recording or screen-target calibration is needed. A missing face clears blink feature history and restarts unfinished calibration; a completed baseline survives ordinary face loss. No manual recalibration during the trial.
- Native raw X = clamp(0.5 + (eye yaw + head yaw)/60); production bypasses gaze EMA. Normalized X is a gaze estimate, not a screen pixel. Before a first scored run, confirm the physical LEFT/RIGHT sign in a separate unscored two-target check with this build and target layout. Freeze the sign before inspecting scored movements. A small, inconsistent or indeterminate separation leaves direction unverified. Never flip a scored trace to make it pass.
- Future live cue delivery must bind the cue file to an observable recorder anchor. Proposed gate: two successive periodic reports with positive capture/AI rates, increasing OS counts and usable Neutral gaze coordinates. Log the exact second report, submitted count N, its receipt and audio request on the same host monotonic clock. The anchor is the precise capture timestamp of the Nth submitted row (one-based), independently checked after import; verify that anchor row has a fresh, published Neutral output. Allow at most 100 ms host request delay after receiving the report. Preserve the entire cue timeline. Reject ambiguous or delayed/buffered readiness (including reports received in a burst), wrong targets, a missed cue, or early playback. Withhold cues if readiness is absent. The anchor is an operational scheduling reference, not an acoustic timestamp. Compute all DSP delays between observations on the recorder clock; do not subtract audio and capture clocks. This live gate is a prerequisite, not a feature of the audio-only rehearsal.
- No recorder clock restart at calibration, cue, or blink. Study times below are seconds relative to the anchor row. Leave enough recorded data for both fixed arrival windows; no shifting windows if data arrive late.

## Integrity, freshness and exclusions

1. Verify executable, mode and parameter header, COMPLETE only, valid footer, zero overflow/configuration changes, paired rows agreeing with submitted/processed counts, ordered unique frame IDs and strictly increasing precise capture timestamps. Reconcile missing capture IDs with console skips, stale packets and evictions. Global integrity failures make both legs inconclusive. A reconciled drop outside a scored interval is reported. A missing ID or adjacent timestamp gap over 100 ms inside the interval invalidates that leg, even if two surviving coordinates match. Stale capture packets discarded upstream have no DSP observation.
2. Gaze carries no native per-frame Tracked/Held flag: input trk=-1. For scoring require exactly 484 packed rows, upstream OK, state 0, actual adapter and DSP call, outcome Ok, status Filtered, a fresh published point (not cached), finite paired raw/filtered X/Y strictly inside (0,1), no reset or generation change. Identical successive coordinates can be fresh. Equality at 0 or 1 is valid production data but saturated for this experiment. Check every publication against the DSP output.
3. Report calibration separately from loss (no face or pose, n=0), extractor error (n=-1), cached/held, timing rejection, initialization, reinitialization, clipped outputs and publication failures. Missing gaze resets the gate and cached output. Reacquisition starts directly at the new observation. State 0 alone cannot establish validity. State 1 = left wink, 2 = right wink, 3 = sustained-closure class. A class is not proof of a particular physical blink. Exclude all non-Neutral and unknown states from scoring, even when the DSP correctly accepted them. A participant-reported interruption overlapping a scored leg also invalidates it if timing is uncertain. Unclassified physiological blinks are not observable from this trace alone.
4. Reuse the stationary protocol's 500 ms wash-in as a **proposed gaze movement safeguard**. After initialization, loss, non-Neutral, discontinuity or other ineligible observation require 500 ms of continuous otherwise-eligible observations before scoring. Start at the first subsequent otherwise-eligible sample, also after any missing frame or >100 ms gap. Do not restart wash-in on each wash-in row. No interpolation, spike removal, replacement interval or cherry-picking.
5. Replay the complete raw DSP-call stream, including accepted non-Neutral samples. Use explicit seconds, independent X/Y, previous filtered position for velocity, chronological derivative history, observed resets, unchanged timing limits and configuration. Compare accepted results to actual DSP and published X/Y, tolerance 1e-12 per axis. Verify bypass/reset/held behavior separately. A mismatch is reported as a numerical/integration discrepancy; withhold a timing verdict until explained. Coverage from the stationary evaluator alone is insufficient for a movement verdict.

## Fixed windows and scoring

All gaze-specific scheduling and adaptations below are **proposals**. The numerical references explicitly identified as borrowed were already used for hand testing; their transfer to gaze requires review.

- For each nominal MOVE M (8 or 20 s), starting reference = [M-2.5,M-0.5); fixed arrival fixation = [M+1.5,M+3.5). Separate local references allow a natural blink between legs. The second reference must be a credible RIGHT fixation: require its raw mean X still lies within max(0.03, 3 × first arrival X SD) of the first arrival mean, and the first arrival must be identifiable/stable, even if the first leg was unscorable for another reason. No reference replacement.
- Require all observations from beginning of that leg's starting reference through the end of its arrival to be eligible, with the 500 ms wash-in satisfied before the reference. Both two-second references require >=25 samples; raw X population SD <=0.01, range <=0.03, absolute ordinary-least-squares X slope <=0.005/s. These are the **borrowed hand fixation limits**, intentionally conservative for gaze. Report actual distributions and failure reason. They are tracking quality gates, not clinical fixation standards.
- Endpoints a and b are mean raw X in the fixed reference and arrival windows. Require signed displacement in the frozen direction, A=abs(b-a)>=0.15, absolute change in mean raw Y <=0.05, as borrowed from hand geometry. Also require return endpoint within 0.05 raw X of starting LEFT mean (**proposed**, to flag a different target or drift). Report raw endpoint values, not physical targeting accuracy. Report head movement if observed; raw gaze includes head contribution and the recorder cannot separate it retrospectively.
- After the first reference ends, use the **first** three consecutive eligible observations all on one side of a by more than max(0.03, 6 × reference SD). Bracket onset by the sample before the run and its first sample. Require confirmation before arrival-window start and correct direction; wrong first departure, no departure or missing coverage = inconclusive. Process chronologically from the start-reference end, including its last observation for the first crossing bracket. If a crossing already occurred before that boundary or cannot be bracketed, report it as unresolved. Never skip a first departure or threshold crossing.
- For both raw and published filtered series, use the same progress (x-a)/(b-a). Record first directional 10%, 50%, 90% crossing brackets [last below, first at/above], recrossings, and number of observed intervals across raw 10%-90%. Search through end of fixed arrival. Report a persistent reversal/second switch before arrival (three samples above 90% followed by three below 50%) as ambiguous input and inconclusive; flag lesser recrossings descriptively. Require raw 90% reached by arrival start and raw settling confirmed by arrival end. Missing or ambiguous raw transition = inconclusive.
- **Proposed gaze-specific interpretation:** a switch between two fixed targets is a step-like input. No hand movement-duration category applies. Raw 10%-90% completed within one sample interval can support a paired **sampled-input-to-filter** delay bracket, provided both sides of each crossing, stable fixations and uninterrupted data are present. Report the true eye rise time unresolved when fewer than three observed intervals span the transition; do not present a hand-speed or physiological-speed pass. At ~20 FPS each sample is about 50 ms. Print timing brackets at native precision for verdicts.
- For raw crossing [r0,r1] and filtered crossing [f0,f1], added delay = [f0-r1, f1-r0]. Preserve negative lower bounds. At both 50% and 90%, borrow the upper limit **200 ms**. For settling, band half-width = max(0.05 × A, 3 × arrival raw SD). If width >0.10 × A, settling is inconclusive. Find the first outside-to-inside entry at/after each series' 90% crossing (same crossing interval allowed) followed by >=500 ms continuously in band by arrival-window end. Bracket entry the same way; borrow added settling limit **250 ms**. Fixed arrival stats and this dwell jointly establish arrival. Report raw and filtered settling times, raw/filtered overshoot, and paired RMS/peak error in X and 2D from the sample preceding raw 10% through the later 90% crossing. Report endpoint bias versus raw at arrival and later drift separately.
- **PASS** requires all gates and every delay upper bound within limit. **FAIL for DSP responsiveness** requires suitable raw input, valid replay, continuous evidence, and a delay lower bound strictly above limit, or filtered failure to cross/settle by the fixed arrival end despite adequate observation time. For an absent filtered event require observations through at least the raw upper event time plus its delay limit (plus 500 ms confirmation time for settling); otherwise inconclusive. **INCONCLUSIVE** for integrity, fixation, target, direction, freshness, sign, calibration, geometry, coverage or ambiguous timing, including a delay bracket straddling a limit. A high raw noise level or missed detection alone is not evidence against DSP. One leg's independent result is retained if the other leg is unscorable.
